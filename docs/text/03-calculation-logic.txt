  Rules every calculation follows
  Every number the platform shows must be traceable to an input, a factor and a formula
  version; these eight rules enforce that.

    1. Activity-based, never spend-based. Use physical quantities from bills (kWh, kVAh,
       litres, kg, tonnes). Rupee amounts are used only for money, never to estimate
       emissions.
    2. Versioned factors. Each factor row stores value, unit, source, version and valid-from
       date. Each result stores the factor row IDs it used.
    3. Recalculation is explicit. When a new factor version arrives (CEA publishes yearly),
       past results stay as published; a recalculated series is shown side by side and labelled.
    4. Location-based Scope 2. Grid electricity uses the national CEA factor. Self-generated
       rooftop solar counts as zero.
    5. Ranges, not false precision. Action savings are shown as low–high ranges; verified
       savings carry an uncertainty band.
    6. Owner confirms inputs. No extracted value enters a calculation until the owner
       confirms it or it passes validation rules.
    7. Per-unit intensity is the main metric. Totals rise when business grows; kWh or kg CO₂
       per tonne of output shows real efficiency.
    8. Estimates are labelled. Anything using a default value (e.g. CBAM precursor defaults)
        is tagged "estimate" on every screen and report.

  Carbon footprint formulas
  The footprint is fuels plus grid power, divided by output; purchased metals are added only
  for CBAM estimates.

  Scope 1 (fuels burned on site):

     S_1 = \sum_{f} Q_f \times EF_f


  Q is the confirmed quantity of fuel f in the month (litres or kg); EF from the factor table.

  Scope 2 (grid electricity, location-based):

     S_2 = \frac{E_{grid,kWh}}{1000} \times EF_{grid}


  Use kWh, not kVAh, from the bill. If the unit has rooftop solar with net metering, grid kWh
  = imported kWh; exported units are not subtracted.

  Scope 3 (only purchased CBAM metals in v1):

     S_{3,metals} = \sum_{m} T_m \times SEE_m


  T = tonnes of pig iron, steel or aluminium bought (from GST invoices, matched by HSN
  code); SEE = supplier's verified value if given, else the EU India default.

  Intensity (the headline metric):

     I = \frac{S_1 + S_2}{P}


  P = tonnes of good castings (or pieces) produced that month. Also show energy intensity:
  kWh per tonne.

  CBAM estimate for an iron or steel good (follows the EU structure; direct emissions only
  are priced for iron and steel (GreenSutra)):

     SEE_{direct} = \frac{S_{1,attributed} + \sum_{p} T_p \times SEE_p}{P}


  Show indirect emissions (S₂ ÷ P) separately for information. Label the result "estimate, not
  verified"; under EU rules up to 20% of a complex good's embedded emissions may rest on
  defaults (GreenSutra). Whether scrap carries embedded emissions is under EU review

  Worked example (illustrative): a foundry uses 90,000 kWh and 1,200 litres of diesel in a
  month and makes 100 t of castings. S₂ = 90 × 0.727 = 65.4 t; S₁ = 1,200 × 2.675 ÷ 1,000 = 3.2 t;
  intensity = 68.6 ÷ 100 = 0.69 t CO₂ per tonne; energy intensity = 900 kWh per tonne, inside
  BEE's 700–2,000 kWh/t range for induction foundries (Down To Earth).

  Baseline and verified savings
  Savings are measured against what the unit would have used at today's production level,
  not against last year's raw bill.

  Step 1. Baseline model (12 months before the first action). Fit energy against production:

     E_m = a + b \times P_m


  E = monthly kWh, P = monthly output. With fewer than 9 months of production data, fall
  back to average intensity (kWh per tonne).

  Step 2. Accept the model only if it fits. Use the monthly thresholds of ASHRAE Guideline
  14: CV(RMSE) at most 15% and net mean bias within ±5%. If it fails, show raw intensity
  trends and label savings "indicative".

  Step 3. Adjusted baseline for each reporting month:

     \hat{E}_m = a + b \times P_{m}^{reporting}


  Step 4. Savings:

     \Delta E_m = \hat{E}_m - E_{m}^{actual} \pm NRA_m


  NRA = non-routine adjustment the owner declares (a new machine, a new shift). Each one
  is logged with a reason.

  Step 5. Convert to money and carbon:

        ₹ saved = ΔE × effective energy rate, where effective rate = energy charges ÷ kWh on
        that bill (fixed and demand charges excluded unless demand fell).
        CO₂ saved = ΔE ÷ 1,000 × 0.727 + fuel saved × fuel factor.

  Step 6. Significance: show "verified saving" only when cumulative savings exceed twice
  the model's standard error over the same months; otherwise "too early to confirm".

  The same logic powers the monthly "₹ saved" message, the lender report and the
  leaderboard, so all three always agree.


  Action savings, payback, loan EMI and solar sizing
  Every recommended action shows a savings range, a payback period and whether a loan
  EMI is smaller than the monthly saving.

  Estimated saving of an action (before it is done):

     \Delta E_{a} = E_{baseline} \times s_{enduse} \times [r_{low}, r_{high}]


  s = share of energy used by the affected equipment (from the sector template or the
  owner's equipment list); r = saving rate range from the action library. Each library row
  cites its source, e.g. foundry measures saving 9–45% of energy in audits (Down To Earth)
  and BEE cluster manuals for capital costs.

  Ranking: sort by simple payback (capex ÷ mid-range annual ₹ saving), zero-cost actions
  first, then CO₂ saved as tie-breaker.

  Loan EMI and the cash test:

     EMI = \frac{L \, r (1+r)^n}{(1+r)^n - 1}


  L = loan amount, r = monthly rate, n = months. The platform shows "cash-positive from
  month 1" when the low-end monthly saving exceeds the EMI.

  Example (illustrative): a ₹10 lakh loan at 8% a year for 5 years (SIDBI green loans have
  been offered at 7.5–8.6% (Mercom)) has an EMI of about ₹20,276. A measure saving
  ₹30,000 a month leaves the owner about ₹9,700 better off every month; simple payback is
  2.8 years.

  Rooftop solar size:

     kW_p = \min\left(\frac{A_{roof}}{a_{kWp}},\; \frac{E_{daytime,yr}}
     {Y_{site}},\; kW_{cap}\right)


  A = usable roof area entered by the owner; a = area needed per kWp; E = yearly daytime
  consumption (10 am–6 pm units if the bill shows them, else an assumed share); Y = site
  yield in kWh per kWp per year from a solar model such as NREL PVWatts for the GPS

  Benchmarks, Green Progress Score and leaderboard
  Rank units by how much they improve against their own past, not by size, so a small
  foundry can beat a large one.

  Benchmark position: energy intensity (kWh per tonne) compared with peers in the same
  sector and cluster. Until 10 peers exist, compare with BEE's published range for induction
  foundries, 700–2,000 kWh per tonne of finished casting (Down To Earth).

  Green Progress Score (0–100):

  Leaderboard rules:

        Rank by % intensity improvement against own baseline, within the same sector and
        cluster.
        Show only when 10 or more units take part; names hidden by default, shown only with
        the owner's consent.
        Show each unit its own rank and the gap to the unit just above it, not the full list.
        Refresh monthly, after bills are confirmed, never in real time.

  Why this design: social comparison cut household use by 2% on average, but by 6.3% for
  the heaviest users and only 0.3% for the lightest (Allcott, 2011). People respond more
  when the comparison point is close to their own level (Callery et al., 2021), hence "gap to

  Time-saved logic
  Time saved is measured in the pilot, not assumed; until then the platform shows the
  formula with clearly labelled starting assumptions.

     H_{saved,yr} = \sum_{t} N_t \times \frac{m_{t,manual} - m_{t,platform}}{60}


  N = number of tasks of type t per year; m = minutes per task done by hand vs on the
  platform.
