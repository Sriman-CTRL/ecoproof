  MSME Carbon Tracking in India: Gap
  Research Report (SDG 13)
    Oct 11, 2026      · ​@Sriman


  Executive summary
  Indian MSMEs want to go green, free carbon calculators already exist, and still fewer than
  1% act: the missing piece is turning a carbon number into a funded, proven saving.

        Size of the problem: 8.84 crore registered MSMEs use about 25% of India's industrial
        energy and produce about 25% of industrial emissions. Energy costs them 5–20% of
        operating expenses, against under 2% for large firms.
        Pressure is real: the EU's CBAM entered its definitive phase in 2026 and the EU plans
        to add about 180 more steel and aluminium products from 2028.
        Intent is high, action is near zero: 66% of MSMEs plan to expand environmental
        policies, yet about 0.8% hold ZED certification and below 0.5% use sustainability
        measurement schemes.
        Existing tools stop too early: SME Climate Hub, ICAI, IIM Bangalore, Vishwa Carbon
        and enterprise ESG software measure or report. None we found links a measurement
        to a loan or proves the saving afterwards.
        What we build: bills in, a ranked savings plan and a loan-ready summary out, then
        monthly proof of savings from later bills. Households use the same engine to size
        rooftop solar under PM Surya Ghar.
        Impact scenario: 1 lakh small manufacturers acting would save about ₹1,800 crore and
        1.6 Mt CO₂ a year.

  Carbon rules are reaching MSMEs:

        27% of India's iron, steel and aluminium exports, worth $8.2 billion in 2022, went to the
        EU (GTRI).
        GTRI expects some exporters to cut steel and aluminium prices by 15–22% to stay
        competitive. Exports of these metals to the EU fell 24.4% in FY25 (AL Circle).
        Large producers often don't share plant-level emissions data with the MSMEs that buy
        steel or aluminium from them, according to GTRI's founder (same source). Downstream
        MSMEs are left without numbers.
        The EU plans to tax about 180 more steel- and aluminium-based products from January
        2028, moving CBAM into manufactured parts, where MSMEs sit (GTRI, April 2026).
        India's own carbon market (CCTS) obliges only about 490 large units, so most MSMEs
        are outside it (SolarQuarter).

  Design rules we take from this: read physical quantities (kWh, litres, tonnes), not rupee
  spend; show every emission factor's source; rank actions by impact and rupees; keep
  users returning with a monthly money benefit, not a one-time score.

  Adoption: how many MSMEs actually act
  About 0.8% of registered MSMEs hold any ZED certification, and about 0.013% reached
  the externally assessed Silver or Gold levels.




  KNN India (Udyam, June 2026; ZED, July 2026); SME Climate Hub India launch (2023). Silver and Gold rounded
  to 11,500.

  Intent is far higher: 66% plan to expand environmental policies (SIDBI–D&B, Apr–Jun
  2025) and 85% planned more measures in late 2024 (Sustainability Online). Research
  puts use of sustainability measurement schemes below 0.5% (Chandra et al., 2026). ZED
  covers quality and environment, not carbon, so it is a proxy; no Indian carbon calculator
  publishes usage figures.

  Downstream data gap (exporters): large steel and aluminium producers often don't pass
  plant-level emissions data to the MSMEs that buy from them (AL Circle, citing GTRI). Our
  records of purchased tonnes, with sourced default factors, give these MSMEs a first
  estimate to share with buyers.


  Proposed solution: how the platform works
  The platform turns bills an MSME already has into a ranked plan, a route to money, and
  monthly proof that the saving happened.




  platform flow · 8 steps, 1 monthly loop

  The highlighted step is what no existing tool does; the monthly loop is what keeps owners
  coming back, which research says one-time calculators fail to do.

  Build for the demo: bill extraction with an LLM plus a manual-entry fallback; a rules engine
  over CEA and IPCC factor tables; action ranking from sector templates; a PDF loan-ready
  summary; a household mode for PM Surya Ghar sizing.


  Usefulness: what an MSME and a household get
  An MSME gets a ranked savings plan and a path to the money for it; a household gets a
  rooftop-solar case with its subsidy.

  Worked example (illustrative assumptions, not measured data): a small unit uses 12,500
  kWh a month (150 MWh a year) at an assumed ₹8 per kWh.

        Grid emissions: 150 MWh × 0.727 = about 109 t CO₂ a year.
        A 15% efficiency saving, below the ~20% found in cluster audits, saves 22.5 MWh:
        about ₹1.8 lakh and 16 t CO₂ a year.
        BEE's cluster programme saw an average payback of about 18 months on such
        measures (BEE).

  For a household

        The same engine reads a home electricity bill and estimates the footprint (300 units a
        month ≈ 2.6 t CO₂ a year at 0.727).
        It sizes a rooftop system under PM Surya Ghar, which pays ₹30,000 (1 kW), ₹60,000 (2
        kW) or ₹78,000 (3 kW) and offers up to 300 free units a month (LAEX summary).
        The money is real: over 12 lakh households earned ₹421 crore selling surplus power in
        FY 2024–25, about ₹3,500 each (Swarajya).

  Expected impact and after-effects
  If 1 lakh units like the worked example act, they save about ₹1,800 crore and 16 lakh t CO₂
  a year; this is a scenario, not a forecast.

  Per unit (section 9 example): ₹1.8 lakh and 16 t CO₂ saved a year.


     Units adopting     Share of registered MSMEs   Money saved a year      CO₂ avoided a year

     10,000             0.011%                      ₹180 crore              1.6 lakh t

     1,00,000           0.11%                       ₹1,800 crore            16 lakh t (1.6 Mt)


  Most registered MSMEs are micro units using far less power than the example, so these
  figures apply to small manufacturers, not the whole registry.

  After-effects beyond the first saving:

        MSMEs: lower costs, a repayment record on green loans, and emissions data that
        helps keep EU buyers.
        Lenders: a pipeline of pre-screened green loans for funds that need deploying, such as
        the $215.6 million GCF–SIDBI facility and the $100 million AFD–SIDBI line (KNN India,
        citing CSEP).
        Policy: the first bottom-up dataset of MSME energy use, which the Ministry does not
        hold today and CEEW recommends building.
        Carbon market (later): verified savings could one day be bundled into CCTS offset
        projects; BEE has an approved methodology for industrial energy efficiency and fuel
        switching (Vision IAS). Whether small units can be bundled is not yet confirmed.
        Households: more rooftop solar uptake toward PM Surya Ghar's 1 crore target.

  Limits of this research: SIDBI–D&B surveys measure what MSMEs say, not what they do.

  local MSME owners would fill that.
