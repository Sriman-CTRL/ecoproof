  01 Target Segment: Which MSMEs to Serve
  First
    Oct 11, 2026        · ​@Sriman


  Decision
  Start with small and medium metal-engineering manufacturers: foundries, forging,
  fasteners and auto components, beginning in Hyderabad's foundry and Balanagar–
  Jeedimetla engineering clusters.

       They pay big energy bills. Energy is 12–15% of a foundry's cost (IIF) and 20–30% in
       energy-intensive MSME sectors (UNIDO). A saving is large in rupees, so the product
       pays for itself.
       They face a deadline. The EU plans to extend CBAM to about 180 downstream
       products, including fasteners and machinery parts, from January 2028 (GTRI). Auto
       components ship about 27% of output to the EU, and at least $1.1 billion of Indian
       exports would come into scope (Outlook Business).
       They are mostly MSMEs. About 90% of India's roughly 5,000 foundries are MSMEs
       (IIF).
       They are reachable. Hyderabad is a listed foundry cluster, and the team can visit units
       in person and work in Telugu.

  Size filter: small or medium category (investment up to ₹125 crore, turnover up to ₹500
  crore under the April 2025 limits), plus larger micro manufacturers, with an own industrial
  power connection, B2B buyers, and 3+ years of operation (the SIDBI green-loan
  minimum).


  Segment scoring
  Metal engineering scores highest because it combines high energy cost, EU carbon
  pressure and a known savings record; textiles come next.

  Why foundry and forging first: they are the only segments where a saving, a loan and a
  buyer deadline all apply at once, which gives three reasons to keep using the product.


  Beachhead: Hyderabad clusters
  Pilot with 30–50 units in Hyderabad's foundry and engineering clusters, where the team
  can visit in person and speak Telugu.

  How to recruit the first units: through the cluster industry association, the SIDBI branch,
  and units already registered for ZED. Ask each for 12 months of electricity bills and one
  buyer contact.

  Success test for the pilot: at least 60% of units upload a second month's bill without
  being chased, and at least 5 units take a loan-ready summary to a lender.


  Expansion order
  Expand from Hyderabad to the clusters with the highest EU exposure before the January
  2028 CBAM expansion.

   1. Now to mid-2027: Hyderabad foundry and engineering pilot; one sector template
      (foundry), then forging.
   2. 2027, before CBAM expansion: Pune, Rajkot, Coimbatore and Faridabad, named as the
      most exposed engineering belts (Outlook Business); Kolhapur, Batala–Jalandhar–
      Ludhiana and Belgaum foundry clusters (IIF).
   3. Next sector: secondary steel, then textile dyeing (Tiruppur, Kolkata), each with its own
      template.
   4. Households: nationwide from launch, because the bill-to-solar flow needs no field
      work.

  Each new cluster needs only a sector template, local tariff rules and a partner association;
  the core platform stays the same.
