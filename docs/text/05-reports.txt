  Why each report wins its audience
  Each report leads with the one number its reader decides on; everything else is
  supporting evidence.

  Lenders: repayment from savings. Lenders want cash-flow comfort. The pack's first line
  is "monthly saving ₹X vs EMI ₹Y" (doc 03), backed by 12 months of DISCOM bills, which
  are third-party documents. RBI is already steering lenders to judge MSMEs on digital
  records, including utility records, through the Unified Lending Interface (RBI Governor,
  June 2026), so bill-based evidence matches where lending is heading.

  EU buyers: avoiding default values. Without supplier data, EU importers must use
  country default values that carry a mark-up (GreenSutra); verified data roughly halves the
  bill (GreenSutra). The buyer's cost logic is:

     Cost_{buyer} = T \times SEE \times Price_{cert} \times Factor_{year}


  Illustration with published figures: 500 t of flat-rolled steel at the India default of 4.28 t
  CO₂ per tonne, at the Q1 2026 certificate price of €75.36 (GreenSutra calculator), is
  €161,270 at the full rate. The payable share is only 2.5% in 2026 (about €4,030) but reaches
  100% by 2034 (same source). Our sheet shows the buyer where the supplier stands now,
  as an estimate, and the path to verified data.

  Indian listed customers: value-chain data. The top 250 listed companies report on value-
  chain partners that make up 2% or more of their purchases, voluntary from FY 2025–26
  (Vinod Kothari); unlisted suppliers are already receiving BRSR-shaped questionnaires
  (GreenSutra). A ready sheet saves the supplier time and makes them easier to keep.

  Report contents
  Field lists for the five reports that leave the platform most often; every field maps to a
  stored value with a data quality tag.

  Loan-ready pack (PDF, 4–6 pages)

   1. Cover: business name, Udyam number, GSTIN, years in operation, site address, report
      ID and date.
   2. Headline box: proposed measure, cost, monthly saving range, EMI at the owner's
      chosen terms, monthly net cash effect.
   3. Energy baseline: 12 months of kWh, kVAh, PF, demand and ₹, each linked to its bill;
      baseline fit statistics.
   4. Bill health findings already fixed or pending, in ₹ per month.
   5. Measure detail: equipment, vendor quotes compared, chosen quote, saving range with
      source, payback.
   6. Carbon impact: CO₂ per year avoided; intensity before and after.
   7. Data quality score and the label "summary for the lender's own assessment".
   8. After the measure: a link that later shows verified savings to the lender (with consent).

  CBAM supplier emissions sheet (Excel + PDF), sections ordered like the EU
  communication template (EU CBAM training):

   1. Installation: name, address, coordinates, contact.
   2. Goods: description and CN code per product, production route (e.g. induction
      melting).
   3. Reporting period: calendar year, as required for goods imported from 2026 (DQS
      template).
   4. Direct emissions: fuels and quantities with factors (doc 03).
   5. Precursors: tonnes of pig iron, steel or aluminium bought, with specific embedded
      emissions and whether each is supplier-verified or a default.
   6. Specific embedded emissions per product (direct; indirect shown separately for
      information).
   7. Share of embedded emissions resting on default values.

  BRSR value-chain sheet (Excel): total energy use (GJ) split into grid, fuels and renewable;

  Monthly report (1 page): ₹ saved this month and to date, kWh per tonne, CO₂ per tonne,
  bill health findings, the next action, and the data quality score.


  Generation pipeline
  Every report goes through the same seven steps, so any number in any document can be
  traced back and verified.

   1. Request: owner (or an approved buyer request) picks a report type, period and
       language.
   2. Snapshot: freeze the inputs, results, factor versions and data quality score into a read-
       only snapshot with an ID.
   3. Compute: run any report-specific calculations (e.g. per-product CBAM figures) on the
       snapshot only.
   4. Render: fill an HTML template and convert to PDF with a headless browser; fill an Excel
      template for spreadsheets. Templates exist in Telugu, Hindi and English; numbers use
       Indian grouping.
   5. Seal: compute a SHA-256 hash of the file, store it with the snapshot ID, and print a QR
      code linking to a public verification page.
   6. Store and share: save the file in object storage; sharing creates a signed link with an
      expiry (30 days by default) and a named recipient.
   7. Log: record generation, every view and every download; the owner sees the log.

  Verification page: anyone scanning the QR sees the report ID, issue date, business name
  and headline figures, plus a check: upload the PDF and the page confirms the hash
  matches. No other business data is shown.

  Regeneration: a report is never edited. A new snapshot makes a new version; old versions
  stay valid with their own hash.

  stronger.
