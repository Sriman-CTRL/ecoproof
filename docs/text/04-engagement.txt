  Principle
  Make the platform a monthly money ritual tied to the electricity bill, not an app people
  open out of compulsion; owners stay because every visit shows rupees they would
  otherwise lose.

       The bill is the trigger. It arrives every month whether or not the owner thinks about
       carbon, so the habit needs no new behaviour, only a forward on WhatsApp.
       Money is the reward. Comparison nudges barely worked where people did not pay
       their own bills (Myers et al., 2020); MSME owners pay theirs, so lead with rupees.
       History is the lock-in. After 12 months, the baseline, loan pack and buyer approvals live
       only here; leaving means losing them.
       The research problem to beat: carbon apps cut emissions at first, then the effect
       fades as use drops (Fosgaard et al., 2024; Enlund et al., 2023). A monthly bill ritual is the
       counter to that fade.


  The core monthly loop
  The whole product is built around one loop that the electricity bill restarts every month.

  Caps and rules:

       No more than 4 messages a month and never between 9 pm and 8 am.
       Only utility templates, sent after opt-in. India's utility rate is about ₹0.145 per message
       from October 2026 (AiSensy), so four a month costs under ₹1 per owner.
       If two messages in a row are ignored, drop to the bill reminder only until the owner
       replies.

  Leaderboard details (scoring rules in doc 03):

       Monthly refresh after bills close; cluster and sector only; at least 10 units.
       Default view: own rank, gap to the next unit, and the "efficient peer" (top-20%
       intensity) as a target.
       Names hidden by default; a unit can reveal only its own name.
       No negative ranks shown publicly; bottom positions are visible only to the unit itself.

  Guardrails
  We aim for a strong habit, not compulsion; these rules protect trust, which is what keeps a
  business owner for years.

       No streak-loss shaming, fake urgency or countdown timers.
       No inflated savings to keep people engaged; every number follows doc 03 and shows
       its data quality.
       No notifications outside the caps above; one-tap opt-out in every message.
       No public ranking without consent; no unit-level data to associations or government.
       No pay-to-rank and no sponsored actions inside the ranked list; vendor offers, if any,
       appear separately and are labelled.
       Consent and deletion follow the DPDP rules (see the platform design tab of the
       research report).
