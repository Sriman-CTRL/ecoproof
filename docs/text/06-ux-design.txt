  Who we design for
  The owner is busy, lives on WhatsApp, distrusts new software, and knows energy as a bill
  amount, not as kWh; design every screen for that person.

  The PayNearby survey covered 10,000+ retail MSMEs; manufacturing owners may differ,
  so the pilot must check these assumptions.

  Five principles:

   1. Rupees first. Every screen leads with ₹; kWh second; CO₂ third.
   2. No typing. Photos, voice notes and one-tap confirms replace forms.
   3. One screen, one job. Each screen has a single primary button.
   4. Show the source. Every number can be tapped to see the bill it came from.
   5. Their language. Telugu, Hindi or English per user, with mixed Telugu–English voice
      accepted.

  Household mode uses three screens: bill upload, solar plan (size, subsidy, saving), and
  after-installation savings.

  Empty states teach: the home screen before the first bill shows a sample foundry's
  results and says "Forward your latest electricity bill to see yours in 2 minutes."


  Interaction rules
  These rules apply to every screen and message; designers and developers check against
  them in review.


     Area                      Rule

  Accessibility and low literacy
  An owner who reads little should still complete the monthly loop by voice and pictures.

       Listen button on the monthly summary and action cards, reading the text aloud in
       Telugu or Hindi using a text-to-speech service (for example Sarvam's Bulbul, listed at
       about ₹30 per 10,000 characters (UsagePricing)).
       Voice in: voice notes for output, fuel and questions, transcribed by Indian-language
       speech-to-text that handles Telugu–English mixing (Sarvam).
       Icons with words, never icons alone; colour always paired with a word ("saved", "loss").
       Text size follows the phone's setting; layouts tested at 130% text size.
       Helper role: an accountant or family member can do every step on the owner's behalf
       with their own login, and the log shows who did what.
