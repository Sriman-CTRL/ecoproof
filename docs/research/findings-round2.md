## 1. The one insight to lead with

**The help already exists. Owners don't reach it, and when they do, they don't follow through.**

- MSE-SPICE (25% capital subsidy for circular-economy machines, up to Rs 12.5 lakh) had **6 users against a target of 3,400+**, and about Rs 2 crore spent of Rs 500 crore (KNN, Oct 2025). [S1]
- About **0.8%** of registered MSMEs hold any ZED certification (our gap report). [S2]
- A randomised trial in 400+ Gujarat plants gave free energy audits. Audits promised about 104% yearly returns, yet treated plants invested only an **insignificant USD 368 more** than others, and only **35%** used the free energy manager offered (Ryan, 2015). [S3]
- What does move small firms: **peer effects, comparison with similar units in the cluster, and first-hand experience** of the technology (Never, 2015, a study of the KfW–SIDBI MSME credit line). [S4]

So EcoProof's role for NGO judges: **the last-mile connector plus the follow-through**. It finds the programme, fills the paperwork, books the hand-off, and proves the result from later bills. This is what the faculty judges asked for, and it is backed by evidence instead of just being a feature.

## Mitra: NGO and government connect pages (approved by Sriman, 13 Oct 2026)

Decisions (Sriman, 13 Oct 17:44 UTC): two pages, **NGO Mitra** and **Govt Mitra**. Help that involves both kinds of body shows on both pages. Pages must be simple and make the owner want to act. Every step costs ₹0 and shows its business gain first. EcoProof issues a **Certificate of Participation** after a confirmed NGO or government interaction that supports SDG 13. It says what was done, how it helps, and carries a few sourced quotes. "Mitra" replaces "Get help" (Mitra = friend; name chosen by Sriman, 13 Oct, over "Saathi"). This changes the 12 Oct "no certificates" rule **only** for participation: EcoProof still never certifies emissions, savings or green status, and the carbon footprint statement keeps its name.

### How NGOs get people to act (and what Mitra copies)

| Technique | Evidence | Mitra design |
|---|---|---|
| Learn by doing in small groups | Farmer Field Schools: participants gained knowledge and adopted better practices, but non-participating neighbours didn't, and large-scale programmes weren't effective (Campbell review, Waddington et al. 2014) [S35] | Small cluster circles (8–12 units) and in-person workshops, not mass messages |
| Show local people who already succeeded ("positive deviance") | Save the Children in Vietnam found local families doing well and had them teach peers, with progress on a public board; malnutrition fell 65–80% over time, reaching 2.2 million people [S36] | "Neighbours who did this": published foundry cases now, real cluster units later |
| Compare with neighbours | Monthly peer-comparison letters cut energy use by about 2% (Allcott 2011, 80,000 households) [S37] | Cluster benchmark once 10 units join (sample data until then, labelled) |
| Small yes first | 76% vs 17% (Freedman & Fraser 1966); weaker for people who don't value consistency (Guadagno et al. 2001) [S40][S41] | A ladder of ₹0 steps; one "next free step" button |
| Recognition, carefully | A *public, performance-based* certificate raised volunteer dropout by about 22% in a BRAC programme in Bangladesh; the same certificate given *privately* had no effect (Islam et al. 2023, J-PAL) [S38] | Certificates are for **participation, not ranking**, and private by default; the owner chooses to share |
| Follow up | Visits without follow-up barely change investment (Ryan) [S3] | Check-ins at 1, 3 and 6 months |
